Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG) is a breakthrough architecture in artificial intelligence designed to make Large Language Models (LLMs) significantly more reliable and accurate. While pre-trained LLMs excel at understanding and generating human language, their static knowledge base limits them. They struggle with proprietary information, lose track of real-time updates, and occasionally generate incorrect facts—a phenomenon known as hallucination.

RAG solves these limitations through a two-step process: retrieval and generation. When a user submits a prompt, the system converts the query into numerical representations called embeddings. It then performs a semantic vector search across external knowledge sources, such as private databases, documents, or live web feeds, to pull the most relevant information. Finally, this retrieved context is fed into the LLM alongside the original prompt.

By grounding its reasoning in verified, real-time data, RAG dramatically reduces hallucinations and eliminates the costly need to continuously fine-tune models. Organizations can update their knowledge bases instantly without retraining the AI, ensuring outputs remain fresh, trustworthy, and contextually precise across technical, business, and domain-specific applications.
